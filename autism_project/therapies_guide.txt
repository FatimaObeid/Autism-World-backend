Guide to Autism Therapies and Educational Support

Several specialized therapies can help support development and improve quality of life for autistic individuals:

Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) focuses on understanding and changing behaviors. It uses positive reinforcement to teach meaningful skills, improve communication, and decrease challenging behaviors. ABA therapy programs are customized to the specific needs of each individual child.

Speech-Language Therapy helps individuals improve their verbal communication, non-verbal expressions, and social pragmatic skills. It teaches children how to articulate words, understand social cues, use visual communication boards, or use alternative augmentative communication (AAC) devices.

Occupational Therapy (OT) assists individuals in developing independent daily living skills. OT focuses on fine motor skills like writing or buttoning clothes, gross motor skills, and addressing sensory processing challenges so children can better navigate their physical environments.

An Individualized Education Program (IEP) is a legal document created for public school students who require special education services. The IEP defines specific academic goals, classroom accommodations, testing modifications, and therapeutic services (like speech or OT) provided directly by the school system.
