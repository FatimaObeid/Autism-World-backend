Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) Core Signs and Levels

Early signs of autism in young children include a lack of consistent eye contact, delayed speech or non-verbal communication, and repetitive behaviors such as hand-flapping, rocking, or spinning objects. Autistic individuals often prefer predictable routines and may experience extreme distress during sudden transitions or unexpected changes in their daily environment.

The DSM-5 classifies Autism Spectrum Disorder into three distinct levels based on the amount of support an individual requires:

Level 1: Requiring Support. Individuals at this level face noticeable challenges with social communication and interaction. They may struggle to initiate conversations, have difficulty making friends, and show inflexible behaviors that interfere with daily life in specific contexts.

Level 2: Requiring Substantial Support. Individuals display marked deficits in verbal and non-verbal social communication skills. Their social impairments are obvious even with supports in place. They have great difficulty coping with change, and their repetitive behaviors are frequent enough to be noticed by casual observers.

Level 3: Requiring Very Substantial Support. Individuals experience severe deficits in verbal and non-verbal communication, often speaking only a few words or remaining completely non-verbal. They show extreme distress when changing activities, and their highly repetitive behaviors severely impair their ability to function independently.
